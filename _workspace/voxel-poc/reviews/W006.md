## Corrected Findings (Initial Rejection)

1. Voxel drink is centered at the vending semantic origin with local bounds
   x/z ±0.25, y 0..1. It lies inside the machine, despite visible=true. Place a
   small 0.25 m-grid drink outside the front body at a plausible dispensing
   height; use a one-cell-wide two-cell-high asset and opaque cap/body palette
   so it is recognizable. Keep its bounds completely ahead of the machine's
   front face. Prove this with relative-space bounds assertions for both modes.
2. `crossingPhaseForOffset` uses epsilon-expanded phase boundaries, but closure
   is not clamped. An offset just before -20 can produce negative closure and an
   offset just before 12 can produce closure above1. Clamp to0..1 and add near-
   boundary assertions plus expected open/closed signed pivot poses and warning
   visibility in both renderers. Comparing two modes alone can hide equal bugs.
3. Browser interaction routing relies on wall-time key holds. Replace with
   bounded polling of actual player coordinates and release keys in finally.
   Root supplied working collision-aware routes. Cover actual prompt click as
   well as E, observe clearing→open after a call, and assert train offset remains
   close/monotonic across a toggle rather than merely unequal. Remove immediate
   reactivation when verifying preservation because that intentionally resets
   the sequence.
4. Result lists unchanged src/scene-spec.ts and calls human visual acceptance a
   root-owned decision. Correct the file list and say human Visual Acceptance
   remains pending, separate from root technical screenshots.

All four findings were corrected and independently rechecked. Visible drink
placement, clamped closure, expected pivot/warning poses, real input/prompt
activation, complete clearing/open progression, and monotonic toggle progression
now have direct evidence. Shared state remains renderer-independent and immutable
scene data survives adapter lifecycle changes.

## Limitations

The two-car train uses one rigid surface seat, as specified in W006, instead of
the PRD's bent-once geometry strategy. This bounded simplification is reported;
the visual follows the closed railway, while scene colliders remain the approved
static contract. It is not a production train collision system.
